How good is the fraud score — and how do we know?

The engine is a rule-based, explainable risk scorer (weighted rules → 0–100 → LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH), not a black box. Accuracy needs ground truth, so this report scores a labelled dataset through the identical logic and evaluates it as a classifier — precision, recall, ROC/AUC and per-rule lift. Data is synthetic but transparent; swap in real investigator-labelled outcomes and the same harness reruns.

Does the score track reality?

The cleanest test of a risk score: within each score band, what share of claims were actually fraud? A useful score climbs monotonically — and this one goes from ~3% at the bottom to 100% at the top.

Ranking quality — ROC

Sweeping the threshold across the whole range traces how true-positives trade against false-positives. The curve hugs the top-left (good); the diagonal is a coin-flip. The two dots are the engine's shipped cut-offs.

The threshold is a business dial

Where you draw the line is a policy choice, not a fact. HIGH is a precise, low-noise queue; MEDIUM catches more fraud at the cost of more false alarms. Pick from the curve by your tolerance for missed fraud vs wasted investigator time.

Actionable finding. DUPLICATE_IMAGE is the star — when it fires, it's fraud 94% of the time. EARLY_CLAIM is the weak link: it fired 70 times but was right only 34% of them, dragging MEDIUM's precision down. The fix is data-driven — demote its weight or reclassify it as a soft signal, and let the image rules carry more of the score.

Method. Rules, weights and thresholds mirror the production engine exactly (FraudEngine.java + *Rule.java). A labelled dataset of 400 claims (13% fraud) is scored through the identical logic; every metric is computed from scratch — ROC AUC via the exact Mann-Whitney form. Reproduce with python fraud_eval.py (seed 42).

Honesty. Labels are synthetic with documented fire-probabilities — this measures the scoring logic on transparent stand-in data, exactly the methodology you'd run on real investigator-labelled outcomes. The engine is rule-based by design (insurance decisions must be auditable); its natural upgrade, once enough real labels exist, is a supervised model on the same features, cross-validated — at which point this harness yields a rigorous accuracy story.
