# Conventional Claims Processing vs ClaimLens: A Side-by-Side

The stages of a claim are the same whether you process it on paper or on a platform. What changes is *who does the work at each stage*, how long it takes, and whether you can prove afterward why a decision was made. Here is the honest comparison.

## Intake

**The old way.** A claim arrives by phone, email, or a walk-in. Someone re-keys the details into a system. Errors from mistyping are common, and nothing stops a claim being filed against the wrong policy until a human notices later.

**The ClaimLens way.** The policyholder files the claim themselves through a portal, against a policy the system already knows. Basic validity — is the policy active on the loss date, does the vehicle match, is this the policyholder — is checked at submission, not days later.

## Reading documents

**The old way.** A person opens each RC book, FIR, estimate and invoice, reads it, and types the key fields into a form. Careful, slow, and the single biggest source of waiting time.

**The ClaimLens way.** OCR reads every document automatically and extracts the fields the process needs — registration number, policy number, amounts. The human reviews and confirms instead of transcribing.

## Spotting fraud

**The old way.** A surveyor eyeballs the photos and trusts their gut. A reused or lightly-edited image looks genuine when you only ever see one claim at a time. Patterns across claims are invisible.

**The ClaimLens way.** Every image is fingerprinted and compared across the tenant's claims; timelines are checked against metadata; document fields are cross-checked against the policy. The system surfaces the handful of claims that actually look suspicious — and leaves the honest majority to flow through.

## Deciding

**The old way.** The reasoning lives in the reviewer's head and a few free-text notes. If someone asks "why was this rejected?" six months later, the answer is often "I don't remember."

**The ClaimLens way.** Every fraud score keeps its per-rule breakdown, and every status change is recorded. The reason a claim was approved or rejected is a permanent, queryable part of the record.

## Scaling

**The old way.** More claims means more reviewers. Throughput is bounded by headcount, and quality drifts as the backlog grows.

**The ClaimLens way.** The routine work — reading, cross-checking, first-pass fraud scoring — is done by the platform. Investigators spend their time on the claims that genuinely need judgement, so the same team handles far more volume without cutting corners.

## What doesn't change

ClaimLens is not "AI decides your claim." A human still owns every real decision. What changes is that the human starts from a claim that has already been read, validated, cross-checked and scored — with the evidence laid out and the suspicious signals flagged.

The old way asks a person to be fast *and* thorough *and* consistent, all day, across a growing pile. That is a lot to ask of anyone. ClaimLens does the mechanical part so the human can do the part only a human should: judge the hard cases, fairly.
